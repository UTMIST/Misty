# Project developer applications

UTMIST recruits project developers each term onto the active project teams. A project
developer builds and ships part of a project under its project lead, rather than
proposing work of their own. This document covers the timeline, who may apply, the
application procedure, the technical assessment, and what the role expects.

## Timeline

Applications open September 8, 2026. The project developer application deadline is
September 29, 2026 at 23:59. Technical assessments are sent within three days of the
deadline and are due one week after they are sent. Offers go out in the second week of
October and the term's work starts immediately after reading week.

A late application is not reviewed. The deadline has been extended once in past years
and that extension was announced before the original date, never after it.

## Who may apply

An applicant is eligible when all of the following hold:

- They are a current student with an active membership for the term.
- They are not already a project developer on another active project.
- They can commit the hours described under Commitment below.
- They have not withdrawn from a project team mid-term in the previous year.

Prior project experience is not required and is not weighed in selection. A first-year
applicant with no portfolio is assessed on the technical assessment alone, which is the
main reason the assessment is weighted as heavily as it is.

A member who is already a project lead may not also apply as a project developer on a
different project, since the two commitments overlap in the same weeks.

## How to apply

1. Choose at most two projects from the open projects list and rank them. An
   application naming more than two projects is returned to the applicant to narrow,
   and the clock does not stop while it is returned.
2. Write a short statement, under 300 words, describing what you want to build. A
   statement that describes a project you would start instead is assessed against the
   project you ranked first, which rarely goes well.
3. List the languages and tools you have used, with a rough sense of how much. Do not
   list a tool you would not be comfortable debugging.
4. Attach a link to code you have written if you have any. A link that requires access
   approval counts as no link, so check it while signed out.
5. Submit before the deadline above. A confirmation arrives within one hour; if it does
   not, the application did not reach the queue and should be resubmitted.

## Technical assessment

The project developer technical assessment is a take-home task of roughly four hours,
sent after the application deadline and due one week later. It is the same task for
every applicant in a given term, and it is deliberately smaller than it looks.

Assessments are reviewed without names attached. The reviewer sees the submission and
the ranked projects, and nothing else from the application. Use of an AI assistant is
allowed and expected to be disclosed in the submission notes; an undisclosed assistant
is treated as a misrepresentation rather than as a tooling choice.

An applicant who cannot complete the assessment in the window for a documented reason
may request one extension of one week, before the due date rather than after it.

## Commitment

A project developer is expected to put in six to eight hours a week during the term and
to attend a weekly project sync. Two consecutive missed syncs without notice releases
the position back to the project, which is the most common reason a position reopens
mid-term.

The role runs for one term and is renewable. A developer who completes a term in good
standing may continue on the same project without reapplying.
